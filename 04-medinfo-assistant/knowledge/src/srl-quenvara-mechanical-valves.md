# Quenvara (davorixaban): use in patients with mechanical heart valves

## Summary

- Quenvara is not approved for patients with mechanical heart valves. The Prescribing Information states that use in patients with prosthetic heart valves is not recommended (section 5.4).
- In the investigator-initiated VALVE-Q pilot study, thromboembolic events were more frequent with davorixaban than with warfarin in patients with a mechanical aortic valve, and the study was stopped early.
- Larkmoor Therapeutics does not recommend Quenvara in patients with mechanical heart valves. Vitamin K antagonists remain the standard of care.

## Background

You requested information on the use of Quenvara (davorixaban) in patients with a mechanical heart valve. This response is provided in reply to your unsolicited request and may include information outside the approved labeling.

## Clinical data: VALVE-Q pilot study

VALVE-Q was an open-label, randomized pilot study in 48 adults who had received a mechanical bileaflet aortic valve at least 3 months earlier. Patients were randomized 1:1 to davorixaban 5 mg twice daily or warfarin (target INR 2.0 to 3.0).

| Outcome at 12 weeks | Davorixaban (n=24) | Warfarin (n=24) |
|---|---|---|
| Thromboembolic events (stroke, TIA, valve thrombosis) | 3 (12.5%) | 0 |
| Major bleeding | 2 (8.3%) | 1 (4.2%) |
| Discontinued study drug | 6 (25.0%) | 2 (8.3%) |

The data and safety monitoring board stopped the study after 12 weeks because of the excess of thromboembolic events in the davorixaban group. No patients with mechanical mitral valves were enrolled.

## Bioprosthetic valves

Patients with bioprosthetic valves were excluded from QUELL-AF. No controlled data are available for Quenvara in patients with bioprosthetic valves.
